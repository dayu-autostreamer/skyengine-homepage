## Our community {#our-community}

SkyEngine was founded by [Dislab](https://dislab.nju.edu.cn/) at [Nanjing University](https://www.nju.edu.cn/). The laboratory's research and development are part of the project's origins.

SkyEngine connects people who want to understand how scheduling decisions behave in a shared factory environment. Contributions can range from a clearer tutorial or a reproducible report to a new simulation scenario or scheduling algorithm.

Meet the project chair and maintainers on the [Committee page](./committee.mdx), and the people contributing to the project on the [Contributors page](./contributors.mdx).

## Find your starting point {#starting-point}

- **Use SkyEngine:** follow the [documentation](/docs/), configure a factory, and run an initial simulation. Use [Support & Contact](./support.mdx) for questions.
- **Improve the system:** contribute simulation, scheduling, experiment, or interface changes to the [SkyEngine system repository](https://github.com/dayu-autostreamer/skyengine). Start with the [developer guide](/docs/developer-guide/) to find the relevant component.
- **Improve this website:** contribute tutorials, translations, navigation, and blog posts to the [website repository](https://github.com/dayu-autostreamer/skyengine-homepage).
- **Help others:** reproduce a problem, review a proposal, explain an example, or share an experiment that others can run.

The [contribution guide](./contributing.md) explains how to prepare and validate a change.

## Research and experiments {#research-and-experiments}

Explore project updates in the [SkyEngine blog](/blog/). For algorithm comparisons, keep factory instances, seeds, disturbances, and simulation limits consistent, and report completion rates alongside completion times. The [experiment workbench](/docs/experiments/platform/) provides training, testing, result comparison, and replay workflows.

For project exchange and research collaboration, see [Support & Contact](./support.mdx#project-contact). [Brand assets](/brand/) are available for project presentations and documentation.

## Working together {#working-together}

Keep discussions respectful and focused on the problem, the evidence, and the proposed improvement. Explain the context of a question, acknowledge others' contributions, and help new participants find their way around the project.
